Fractional CHRO

A leadership team held a retreat six months ago. Everyone loved it. Nothing was different by the following Tuesday.

That's not a story about a bad retreat. It's what happens to almost all of them. People leave a room with real intentions to behave differently, and then Monday happens: a customer needs something, someone quits, a deadline moves. The intentional work loses to the urgent work, every time, unless someone is there afterward to catch it when it does.

I'm Wendy Dawson. I work as a fractional CHRO for companies who need more than a framework delivered once. Before I schedule anything, I talk to every person on the leadership team privately, so by the time we're in a room together, I'm not running a curriculum — I'm working on what's actually happening inside this specific team. And when the room ends, I don't leave.

Most engagements end when the offsite does. Mine begins there.

What's actually different

Most facilitators open by scheduling the workshop. I open by interviewing every leader privately first — their history, where the team is stuck, what nobody says out loud when the group is together. By the time we're in a room, I already know what's real.

Then the room isn't a workshop. It's a business meeting. I put a genuinely stuck decision on the table and we work it, live, using what we just learned about each other.

Then I stay. I sit in real meetings afterward and name behavior as it's happening, because that's the only place intentional change survives contact with an actual Tuesday.

For the operational side of HR

Recruiting, compliance, day-to-day administration — that's the work of The Dawson Group, and I'll point you there directly when that's what you need. What I do here is the part that doesn't get outsourced: the judgment, the diagnosis, and staying long enough to know it held.
