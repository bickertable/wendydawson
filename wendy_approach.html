Approach

The work happens in three moves, and none of them are a workshop delivered once.

This is the actual shape of an engagement, in the order it happens.

01

Before anyone books a room

I talk to every person on the leadership team privately, before anything is on the calendar. I'm listening for the history between them, where they're actually stuck, and what nobody says when the group is together. By the time we're in a room, I have a map of this team's real dysfunction — not a generic one.

02

The room is a business meeting, not a workshop

I believe in the offsite. It's the only setting focused enough for this work. But I don't teach a model in the abstract and send everyone home with good intentions. I put a real, stuck business decision on the table — one the team has been unable to move on for months — and we work it live, using what we just covered, in front of the actual disagreement.

Your team leaves the offsite with a decision it couldn't make before it.

03

Then I stay

Doing business gets in the way of doing business. That's the entire reason most leadership development doesn't hold — the intentional work loses to whatever's urgent that week. So instead of ending when the room ends, I stay embedded: in the real meetings, naming behavior as it happens, coaching leadership privately on being the example rather than the exception.

What starts in your executive room becomes how your whole organization runs.

Who this isn't for

I won't work with a leader who treats people as a means to a KPI rather than the reason the KPI gets hit.

If the theory of success is driving people harder and meaner, this isn't the right engagement. The work only holds for a leader willing to be coached in front of their own team.
